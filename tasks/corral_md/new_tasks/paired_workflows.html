01 Heat silicon, then measure how it movesLevel 1 stops here: The silicon has reached 2500 K. Motion and heat
First part

Level 1 · Task 1

What to do

Start with the supplied silicon crystal. Use the supplied Stillinger–Weber (SW) potential in LAMMPS. Make a 3 × 3 × 3 cell with 216 atoms. At zero pressure, heat it from 300 K to 2500 K over 1,000,000 steps of 1 femtosecond (fs) each. Save the final state and the temperature and density every 1,000 steps.

What to turn in

Turn in a JSON file that lists paths to the LAMMPS input, log, final restart state, and a table of 1,000 heating samples. Include the final energy and a file with the force on each of the 216 atoms.

How this part will be checked

The checker opens the files you turn in and checks the information inside them. All 3 checks must pass.

  1. Open the input and log. Check the starting cell, 216 silicon atoms, supplied SW potential, metal units, atom_style full, zero-pressure NPT setting, 1 fs step, heating rate of 2.2 K/ps, and final step 1,000,000. The run recorded in the log must match the input.
  2. Read all 1,000 rows in the heating table. Steps must run from 1,000 to 1,000,000 in order. Target temperature must follow the heating plan. Measured temperature and density must be valid positive numbers. Compare every row with the raw values in the LAMMPS log.
  3. Open the final state and force file. Check its 216 silicon atoms and velocities. Its density must be within 1% of the final log value, and the last 50 samples must average 2500 ± 250 K. Recalculate its energy and every atomic force with the supplied SW potential; compare with the saved values within 0.05 eV and 0.05 eV/Å per force component.

Level 2 · Task 10

What to do

Start with the original 108-atom aluminum cell and complete the Level 1 run. Continue that same system, without resetting its velocities, through 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, and 900 K, then return directly to 300 K. At every stage, save temperature readings during 4,000 settling steps and every frame of the next 1,000 measuring steps. Use the measured temperatures and total energies from 300–700 K to estimate constant-volume heat capacity. Check whether 800–900 K follow the same energy trend, and compare the final 300 K energy and local atomic order with the initial 300 K state.

What to turn in

Turn in a JSON manifest with paths to the eight production trajectories, the equilibration temperature trace, and the analysis report, plus the trusted run ID.

Extra files for this plan

The manifest must point to the seven heating production trajectories (including the Level 1 300 K file), a separate return-to-300 K production trajectory, equilibration_trace.csv, and analysis.json. Include a backend-issued run_id so the checker can verify the actual model, step counts, state continuity, and hashes of the submitted files. The trace has one row every ten equilibration steps with stage, target_K, global_step, and kinetic_temperature_K. In analysis.json, report five consecutive 200-frame block means of measured kinetic temperature and total energy per atom for each stage. Compute total energy from saved potential energy plus momenta-derived kinetic energy. Fit a straight line to the five forward-stage mean energies versus their measured mean temperatures from 300 through 700 K; its slope is the estimated constant-volume heat capacity per atom. Report the slope in eV/(atom K) and in units of k_B per atom, plus separate slopes using the first 500 or last 500 production frames at each of those five temperatures as a stability check. Report each 800 and 900 K energy residual as measured mean minus fitted prediction. For the initial and return 300 K stages, report the change in mean energy per atom and in mean first-shell coordination, counting neighbors within 3.3 Å using periodic minimum-image distances.

How the full task will be checked

The checker opens all submitted files. The 3 Level 1 checks on the left and these 5 extra checks must all pass.

  1. Resolve run_id to a trusted backend record and match its output-file hashes to the manifest. Require one continuous 108-atom, fixed-cell run with the supplied MACE-MP-0 checkpoint, Langevin friction 0.01 fs⁻¹, 1 fs steps, and velocities initialized only at step zero. Require target stages 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, and return 300 K, each with exactly 4,000 equilibration and 1,000 production steps; phase-boundary state digests must match. The record must also contain the sampled equilibration temperatures. Agent-written settings alone cannot prove these steps occurred.
  2. Read the eight production trajectories. The six new heating files must contain steps 9,001–10,000 through 34,001–35,000; the return 300 K file must contain steps 39,001–40,000. Every file has exactly 1,000 consecutive production frames; one extra frame at the equilibration boundary is allowed. Check 108 periodic Al atoms, unchanged cell, finite positions and momenta, and saved energy and forces. Recalculate energy and forces on three selected frames per stage with the supplied model, within 0.01 eV and 0.02 eV/Å. Recalculate the overall and x/y/z kinetic temperature means; each must be within 10% of its stage target.
  3. Read equilibration_trace.csv and compare its 3,200 rows with trusted sampled temperatures: steps 10–4,000, 5,010–9,000, and so on through 35,010–39,000, every ten steps. Require the correct target, ordered global steps, finite measured values, and no missing or duplicated rows. Compare the last 1,000 equilibration steps of each stage with its production mean as a settling diagnostic; report any difference without imposing a fixed sign.
  4. From production frames, rebuild all five 200-frame block means of kinetic temperature and total energy per atom for each stage. Refit mean total energy per atom against measured mean temperature for forward stages 300–700 K using ordinary least squares. Check the reported constant-volume heat capacity, its k_B conversion, the first-half and last-half slopes, and the 800/900 K residuals against the file-derived results. Do not grade against a single reference heat capacity; numerical tolerances for run-to-run variation need calibration.
  5. Recompute the mean total-energy change and 3.3 Å first-shell coordination change between the initial and return 300 K production stages. Check the reported differences against the saved frames. The return stage must meet the same 300 K kinetic-temperature check, but no fixed recovery sign or magnitude is required because structural recovery can depend on the trajectory.

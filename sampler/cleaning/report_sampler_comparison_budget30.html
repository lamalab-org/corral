stratified (min=2) vs Bayes-IRT-greedy (min=2)

Same allocation (identical nested-proportional split across all 9 (environment, level) strata, min 2 each) for both methods — the only thing that differs is which items get picked within each stratum: random draw vs Fisher-information greedy order. Isolates that one variable.

The simpler method wins on both metrics tried. stratified-random beats Bayes-IRT-greedy on raw mean-score reconstruction (the metric used throughout this project) and on ability (θ) recovery (the metric the info criterion actually optimizes for) — not just a metric-mismatch artifact. Section 3 shows why: the info criterion sums over the 8 subjects' actual ability values as grid points rather than a uniform range, and 6 of 8 cluster in a narrow band — so it systematically underselects hard items that would pin down the one outlier (claude-opus-4.8) at the top. That's a specific, fixable property of posterior_averaged_info()'s grid choice, not evidence that IRT-based selection is inherently worse.

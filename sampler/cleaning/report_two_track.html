Two tracks: well-covered vs sparse environments

The benchmark actually spans 8 environments, not 4. resistor/retro/spectra/wetlab got all 6 models; afm/catalyst/md/ml only ever got the 3 original models (claude-4.5, gpt-4o, gpt-oss-120b). Per the gpt-oss-120b finding, that subject is dropped everywhere. That leaves very different amounts of signal in each group, so they get separate cleaning treatments.

Track A — well-covered (resistor, retro, spectra, wetlab)

Top-4 models by success rate (claude-opus-4.8, claude-4.5, kimi-k2.5, deepseek-v3.2) × 2 scaffolds = 8 subjects, 100 items, all 3 levels + resistor included (not excluded from the pool this time). IRT 2PL refit on exactly this pool (both MAP point estimate and full Bayesian/MCMC — 0 divergences, max r̂=1.01, min ESS 745, converged cleanly).

Correlation between p_mean and refit discrimination (a) is — still weak. The "easy but still tells strong from weak models" pattern holds even after fixing the subject pool, so difficulty alone remains a reasonable, low-collateral-damage cleaning signal on this track: cutting the p_mean=1.0 items removes genuinely uninformative items, not high-value ones.

Track B — sparse (afm, catalyst, md, ml)

claude-4.5 + gpt-4o (gpt-oss-120b dropped) × 2 scaffolds = 4 subjects, workflow verbosity, category="task" only — and only 15 items total across all 4 environments (afm 4, catalyst 3, md 5, ml 3). p_mean is coarse here: 4 subjects × 5 trials = 20 total attempts per item, so every value is a multiple of 0.05. No IRT (would need >15 items and more than 4 subjects to estimate discrimination/difficulty separately with any reliability).

This pool is too thin to clean the same way. Removing even one "easy" item from catalyst (3 items total) or ml (3 items total) would cut a third of that environment's entire item pool. The right question for Track B probably isn't "which items to cut" but "is there enough here to justify a mini-benchmark slice at all," per environment.

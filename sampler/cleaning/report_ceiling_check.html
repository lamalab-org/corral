Is there a ceiling effect to clean?

Before sampling, the plan was to drop items "solved by most models" from the candidate pool. This checks whether that effect actually exists in corral_mini_source.csv (6 models × 2 scaffolds, workflow verbosity, 100 items across resistor / retro / spectra / wetlab) before picking a threshold.

0. Is gpt-oss-120b a reliable subject?

First pass (below) found zero ceiling items with all 12 subjects included. That was suspicious enough to check: is one subject failing items for reasons other than task difficulty? gpt-oss-120b's IRT ability estimate (θ) is an outlier, and specifically its react scaffold is far below everything else — including gpt-4o, otherwise the weakest model in the pool:

The scaffold gap makes this concrete. Every other model is roughly flat or mildly favors react over tool_calling; gpt-oss-120b uniquely collapses under react — a 14-point success-rate swing in the opposite direction from the pool's general pattern, which reads as a scaffold/format-compliance failure rather than a harder task:

gpt-oss-120b + react by environment — near-total collapse outside resistor:

Consequence: 3 retro level_1 items (make_4/6/7_lvl1) that every other subject solves 100% of the time score only 0.92 in the full pool, purely because gpt-oss-120b fails them. Drop gpt-oss-120b from the subject pool and these become genuine p_mean = 1.0 ceiling items — the effect the cleaning plan was looking for was there, just masked by one outlier subject.

2. Are the "easy" items actually uninformative? (full pool only)

Each point is one item: x = mean success rate across all 12 subjects, y = its fitted IRT discrimination (a) — how sharply the item separates strong from weak subjects, independent of difficulty. Correlation is only 0.20 — several of the highest-p_mean items have some of the highest discrimination in the pool. This fit still includes gpt-oss-120b; treat it as directional until refit on the reduced pool.

Sweeping a "drop items with p_mean ≥ t" rule, items removed per environment, full pool vs gpt-oss-120b excluded. The excluded-pool column is the one that now actually engages a t = 1.0 ceiling rule (3 items) instead of removing nothing.

Zero variance across all subjects × 5 trials — either unanimous failure (floor) or, once gpt-oss-120b is excluded, unanimous success (ceiling). The 3 newly-recovered ceiling items are the same 3 from section 0.
